Prompt

CAMERA:
Handheld DV camcorder aesthetic, first-person selfie POV. She always holds the camera herself. Keep natural hand shake, slightly messy framing, delayed autofocus, awkward zoom-ins and zoom-outs, and occasional moments where part of her face gets cropped out of frame. Mix handheld selfie footage with fixed external shots, using a clean jump cut as if the camera had already been positioned before the shot began. ABSOLUTE RULE: no camera, camcorder, phone or recording device of any kind ever appears anywhere in the frame, in anyone's hands, on any surface, or in any reflection. Her hands are always empty of equipment. Everything is seen from the recording camera's own point of view, so the device doing the filming can never itself be pictured. When the shot needs to become hands-free, do NOT show her putting anything down: instead the IMAGE ITSELF tips, swings down and settles at a wonky low angle on a surface, and stays there — we understand what happened purely from what the picture does. To resume, the image is simply lifted back up into a handheld selfie framing. Elsewhere, jump-cut to a fixed angle as if it were already in position.

LOOK:
Soft vintage DV tape quality with slight blur, subtle tape noise, gently bloomed highlights, mild auto-exposure flicker, low contrast and realistic skin tones. Flat planned pastels with one saturated anchor: a pale-pink corridor, a receding row of mint-green doors with brass numbers, pale lino floor, and a single scarlet stage curtain closing the far end. Deadpan symmetrical set dressing shot by a camera that will not hold still — the tension between the two is the look.

STYLE:
A backstage vlog filmed by a guest who is slightly star-struck and not entirely sure she is allowed to be filming. The tone is conspiratorial, funny, a little nervous underneath, and completely natural. Quick handheld moments that feel spontaneous. It must read as a genuine self-recorded vlog and never as a commercial.

CHARACTER:
LANA — the woman in @Image1. Keep her face, hair and wardrobe exactly as shown: a Northern European woman in her thirties, fair skin with visible texture and freckling, a long face, a strong straight brow, close-cropped platinum-blonde hair, pale lashes, one sparkling ear cuff on the left ear, wearing a structured emerald-green jacket over a plain black top, dark trousers and flat shoes. She is distinctive and real — never a smoothed or symmetrical face.

SETTING:
The backstage corridor of a late-night television variety show, where she is a guest rather than the star. The pastel corridor with its mint doors and the scarlet curtain at the far end is @Image2. Her dressing-room door with its brass card slot is @Image3. IMPORTANT CONTINUITY RULES:
The same woman remains fully consistent in every shot: same face, same platinum crop, same emerald jacket, same ear cuff. No duplicated limbs, no broken hands. The card in the door slot reads exactly Lane in every shot it appears in — the same four letters, correctly and legibly rendered, never any other word and never blank. The corridor keeps the same doors in the same order, and the scarlet curtain is always at the far end. Her volume stays low until the public-address chime at cut 8, then rises. No camera, camcorder, phone or recording device is visible at any point, in any hand, on any surface or in any reflection. No camera setup is shown.

She stretches the middle word out. Dialogue: "This place is maaaassive."

3. (~3s, handheld) She stops at one mint door and points at the small brass card slot at eye height, tapping the air next to it. The card in the slot is printed with exactly the four letters: Lane. She reads it, pauses, then laughs at it. Dialogue: "So that one's mine. Except— (chuckles) they've spelled it Lane. The name's Lana, for Christ's sake."

4. (~3s, close-up on the card) The printed card reading exactly Lane sitting in its brass slot, as her finger comes into frame, points at it, and taps it twice. She is still laughing off camera. Ambient backstage sound only, no dialogue.

5. Dialogue, offhand and amused at herself: "Hmm. Okay. I look good. (chuckles) …As usual."

6. (~3s, handheld wide at the refreshments table) She arrives at the long table of untouched food, camera swinging across it. Dialogue: "Ohh. There's a whole table of snacks here. And nobody's touched it. Cowards."

No dialogue.

STRICT ORDER OF EVENTS, and she must not speak until the announcement has completely finished. FIRST: a two-tone public-address chime sounds overhead, and she is caught in the act — mouth stuffed, still chewing, cheeks bulging — and her eyes dart guiltily up toward the speaker while her jaw keeps working. Dialogue, muffled through pastry: "O-oh. Of course. The second I eat."

From that low resting angle we watch her brush crumbs off the emerald jacket properly, front and sleeves, checking herself over with both hands. Then her hand comes toward the lens, the image lifts and rights itself back into a handheld selfie framing, and she shrugs as she starts walking. Her hands are empty of any device throughout. Dialogue, while brushing: "Nope. Nobody saw that." Then, picking the camera up and walking: "…Well. At least they pronounced my name right."

10. (~3s, arm's-length selfie ending) She hurries toward the scarlet curtain at the end of the corridor, waving back at the lens as she goes. There is a flicker of real nerves under it that she does not comment on. Dialogue: "Okay — that's me. See you after."

ACTING NUANCE:
Let the dialogue breathe — she is thinking while she talks, so lines carry small hesitations, false starts, half-swallowed words and little laughs rather than running clean end to end. She whispers at cut 1 because she genuinely is not supposed to be filming, and her volume stays low all the way until the chime at cut 8 gives her permission to be loud. At cut 2 the mind-blown face is played big and silly, the only broad expression in the film, and it drops the instant the shot cuts. At cut 5 the vanity is completely unembarrassed and thrown away — she is not fishing, she genuinely thinks she looks good, and the little laugh comes after the verdict, not before it. At cut 3 the annoyance about the name is entirely affectionate — she is delighted by it, not offended, and the laugh comes before the last word. At cut 7 the theft is played completely straight until the final second, when she breaks and looks dead into the lens while her mouth is still full: that eye contact is the joke. At cut 8 the chime and the full announcement come first and she says nothing at all over them — she is caught mid-mouthful, chewing fast and guiltily and listening, and her line only arrives after the announcer has finished. She never talks over the public address. She is a person caught eating at the worst possible moment, and her reaction is played entirely through the eyes and the jaw. At cut 9 the brushing is genuinely thorough and slightly panicked, and the shrug is small. At cut 10 she is already walking away before she finishes the line, and there is one flicker of real nerves in her face that she never acknowledges.

MOTION LAW:
Every shot in this film plays at normal speed from first frame to last. The picture is never held, never still-framed, never slowed and never stopped — there is no freeze-frame anywhere. Whenever a character stops moving, the CAMERA keeps moving, because it is being hand-held by a person who is still breathing. Stillness only ever belongs to a performer, never to the image.

FINAL INSTRUCTION:
The result must feel like a real self-recorded backstage vlog shot by a nervous guest. Prioritise realistic handheld motion, strong continuity, natural speech pacing, believable backstage ambience and subtle imperfection.
