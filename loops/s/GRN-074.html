Prompt

FORMAT
Exactly 24 seconds, vertical 9:16, one continuous uninterrupted take, no cuts. Authentic found footage: a real prank filmed at night in 2004 on a cheap consumer MiniDV handycam. Fast, punchy pacing with no dead pauses. It must look like a genuine home recording, not a film and not a modern phone video.

CAMCORDER IMAGE (MANDATORY)
Standard-definition MiniDV look, about 480 lines upscaled: soft image, interlacing combing on fast motion, mild sharpening halos, low dynamic range, ceiling lights and steel reflections blown to white, shadows crushed to black with chroma noise, warm yellow white balance, slow auto-exposure and autofocus. No HDR, no smartphone sharpness, no cinematic grading, no shallow depth of field. People, skin, fabric, steel, and concrete look physically genuine through this imperfect image. No REC text, date stamp, or any overlay.

HANDHELD OPERATION
Held by an unseen friend. Natural hand shake that grows with emotion: sway while hiding, bouncing while laughing, violent jolts during the fall, trembling at the reveal. Tilted horizon, framing that drifts and corrects late, focus that hunts before locking, clumsy zooms that overshoot, heads sometimes cut off by the frame edge. The operator, his hands, body, shadow, reflection, and the camcorder NEVER appear on screen, including in steel doors and walls. Only his voice is heard, close to the mic.

LOCATION
Old apartment building corridor at night, 2004: yellowed beige walls with scuffs, worn grey tile floor, warm ceiling lights, one fluorescent tube flickering down the hall. Brushed steel elevator landing doors with a steel sill. On the wall right of the doors: a call button and, taped above it, a white A4 sheet with large bold black letters "OUT OF ORDER". Above the doors: a small red digital display showing "7". Cabin interior: scratched brushed steel walls, cold white ceiling light, rear handrail, button panel on the right front wall.

CHARACTERS
OPERATOR: never seen, voice only, young man, excited whisper, loud laugh.
FRIEND B: man, about 24, blond curly hair, denim jacket, white T-shirt, grey jeans, a silver flip phone in his right front pocket.
JAKE: man, about 25, short dark hair, light grey hoodie, black backpack on one shoulder, white sneakers, wired earbuds, texting on an open silver flip phone.
Ordinary real-looking people, natural skin with imperfections, consistent faces and clothing throughout.

THE PRINT
A thin vinyl sticker cut to exactly the size of the cabin floor, wall to wall, no white border, nothing extending into the corridor. It shows a photo of a shaft looking down and from a distance convincingly looks like a missing floor. Up close, gloss and faint creases reveal it is flat. The image stays identical whenever visible.

THE REAL SHAFT
Seen only at the end: irregular and physically real, rough stained concrete, a steel guide rail with rusty brackets, snapped greasy cables, dust. Never symmetrical, never a repeating tunnel, no visible bottom, no lit end. Completely unlike the print.

TIMELINE
0.0-2.5 seconds:
Shaky shot straight into the open elevator: the floor seems missing, a deep shaft below. The operator giggles. Friend B steps into frame, plants one foot on the "void", the vinyl crinkles, he bounces once and grins at the lens: "Looks real, right?" He steps back out into the corridor.

2.5-4.5 seconds:
The camera whips to the wall right of the doors; focus locks on the "OUT OF ORDER" sheet. Friend B rips it off, crumples it, and stuffs it into his right front pocket: "Can't have that ruining it." He presses the call button to hold the doors open, and both flatten against the wall left of the doors. The camera settles beside the doorway, aimed into the cabin.

4.5-8.0 seconds:
Jake walks in from the right, texting on his flip phone, and steps into the cabin. His foot lands on the printed shaft: he screams "WHOA!", stumbles back two steps, and slams his back and backpack into the rear wall, grabbing the handrail. On that impact the whole cabin drops about ten centimeters with a heavy metallic CLUNK and tilts slightly, leaving a clear visible step between the cabin floor and the corridor sill. The cabin light flickers. Jake freezes, wide-eyed. No frozen T-pose, no arms held out straight.

8.0-11.0 seconds:
The operator bursts out laughing, thinking the clunk was part of the joke; the camera bounces. Friend B points into the cabin: "Bro, your face!" A clumsy zoom on Jake overshoots and cuts off his head. Jake sees it is a sticker, goes red, and slaps a button: "You guys are idiots." The doors slide closed and meet fully. The display still shows "7".

11.0-12.0 seconds:
Only after the doors are fully closed, the display changes to "6" with a motor hum. Friend B mimics Jake's scream.

12.0-15.0 seconds:
A massive metallic BANG behind the doors; the doors shudder in their frame. A sharp crack of a snapping cable, then the whip of loose cable. The display goes black. Instantly a piercing continuous steel-on-steel SCREECH: the cabin's emergency brakes grinding on the guide rails as it falls. It starts harsh right behind the doors, making them vibrate, then travels rapidly downward, more muffled, lower in pitch, hollow and echoing, with irregular knocks of the cabin hitting brackets. The corridor lights dip. The camera is thrown toward the tile, the mic clipping, then swings back to the doors, out of focus.

15.0-16.5 seconds:
The screech ends far below in a deep heavy CRASH; the floor shudders. A short echo rolls up the shaft, then silence except the fluorescent buzz and shaky breathing. Focus finds Friend B's face, half cut off by the frame, pale. Operator whispers: "Dude..."

16.5-18.5 seconds:
Friend B jams his fingers into the door seam and drags the doors apart with his whole body, sneakers slipping on tile. They grind open to shoulder width. Behind them: pure black.

18.5-20.5 seconds:
A click: the camcorder's small built-in light switches on, a harsh cold beam with fast falloff. The camera pushes to the gap over Friend B's shoulder. No cabin. The beam shows only the nearest few meters: stained concrete, a rusty bracket, snapped cables swaying and knocking the wall, dust in the beam, grit sprinkling onto the lens. The camera tilts down: the light dies after two floors, below is total black.

20.5-24.0 seconds:
Friend B pulls his flip phone from his right front pocket; the crumpled sign comes out with it, drops onto the tile beside the sill, and half unfolds. The camera tilts down and focus locks on the bold words "OUT OF ORDER". Above frame, Friend B flips the phone open, dials, and whispers: "...Jake?" A beat. From deep in the black shaft, faint and echoing, a silly polyphonic cartoon quacking ringtone starts playing. The camera stays on the sign at the edge of the shaft, trembling. The ringtone keeps playing. Take ends. No title, no further event.

PHYSICS AND REALISM
Real weight and inertia: Jake's stumble and backpack impact, the cabin's sudden ten-centimeter sag and tilt, laughter shaking the camera, finger strain and heavy door resistance, sneaker slip. Crumpled paper falls and unfolds naturally. Cables sway with decaying momentum; dust and grit fall with gravity. Light comes only from corridor practicals, the cabin light while the cabin is present, the camcorder light, and the phone screen.

AUDIO AND DIALOGUE
Camcorder onboard mic: close, thin, compressed, handling rustle, faint tape hiss, clipping on loud sounds. Vinyl crinkle, paper ripping and crumpling, whispers, footsteps, scream, backpack on steel, the cabin clunk, echoing laughter, button click, door motor, lift hum, the bang, cable snap and whip, the long brake screech traveling down with echo and knocks, the deep crash, fluorescent buzz, doors grinding, hollow shaft air, creaking cables, grit on the lens, and the distant echoing ringtone. The ringtone is an original simple quack melody, not any existing song. English dialogue only, naturally lip-synced: "Looks real, right?" / "Can't have that ruining it." / "WHOA!" / "Bro, your face!" / "You guys are idiots." / "Dude..." / "...Jake?" No music, narration, subtitles, or laugh track.

STRICT CONTINUITY
Two visible people plus the unseen operator. Jake is never seen after the doors close. The sticker stays in the cabin and leaves with it. The sign is torn off once, stays in the pocket, and falls out only at the end. The display changes only after the doors fully close and stays black after failing. One sag, one fall, one door opening. The cabin never returns. The real shaft never resembles the print. Nobody steps into the shaft. No smartphones, only flip phones. No blood, no bodies, no injuries, no visible sparks, no explosions, no extra people, no duplicated limbs. The operator and camcorder never appear in reflections or shadows.
