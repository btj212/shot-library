[Generation goal]
Generate a 30-second commercial for a chocolate maker, staged as a full courtroom drama about who ate the last chocolate. The genre is played completely straight at every moment.

[Global setting]
A wood-panelled courtroom: deep walnut, a raised bench, a gallery of onlookers, dust turning in a shaft of high side-light. Deep gold and red packaging is the only warm colour against the walnut.

[Camera]
Full courtroom-drama coverage played with total sincerity: low angles on the bench, a long walk-and-stop for the prosecutor, tight ECUs on the accused, reaction cuts to the gallery, and one rack focus at the end. Hard cuts, dense and escalating.

[Motion law]
Every shot plays at normal speed from first frame to last. The picture is never held, still-framed, slowed or stopped, and there is no freeze-frame anywhere. When a character stops moving, the CAMERA keeps living — stillness belongs to a performer, never to the image. Where the film calls for calm at the end, that calm comes from the action genuinely finishing, never from footage slowing down.

[Performance]
Every performer plays it as if a serious crime is being tried. Nobody winks. The accused is genuinely frightened. The gallery genuinely gasps.

Primary event: full coverage — panelling, a low angle on the bench, a gavel, dust in a shaft of light.
Dialogue language: British English, formal and completely serious.
