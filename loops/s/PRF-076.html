Prompt

ONE SINGLE CONTINUOUS SHOT — a 30-second take in 4:3, NO cuts: one camera that starts locked-off and then travels one slow half-circle dolly around a barber chair, with the punchlines delivered by moving CLOSE to details, never by cutting. A small old barbershop in a single sage-green world: sage painted walls, cream wainscoting, worn terracotta tile floor, one bentwood waiting bench, glass bottles of green tonic on a shelf, soft morning light through the shopfront glass.
In the barber chair, wearing the full striped barber cape, sits a large leafy TOPIARY BUSH. Behind it stands the barber: an older heavyset man, white moustache, putty smock, sleeves rolled. On the bench a middle-aged man in a sage cardigan waits with a potted fern on his lap, reading a folded newspaper. Everyone treats all of this as completely ordinary.
0-6s: static wide; the barber shakes out the cape, tucks it at the bush's 'neck', pumps the chair up twice with two heavy clunks, and asks flatly: "The usual?" — the bush answers with one small polite rustle. The waiting man turns a page.
6-13s: the camera begins its slow half-circle; the barber mists the bush from a brass atomizer — close on the leaves beading with droplets — then combs it with a wide wooden comb, the foliage parting in neat tracks like hair.
13-20s: the trim: precise scissor-work in a quick confident rhythm, leaf clippings falling into a tidy cone on the terracotta tiles; close on the scissors flashing; close on the barber's unhurried, professional squint; the fern on the bench trembles very slightly, watching.
20-26s: the reveal: the barber spins the chair to face the mirror — the bush has been trimmed into a PERFECT POODLE silhouette, pom-poms and all; a held beat of silence; then the poodle-bush shakes itself exactly like a wet dog, shedding two leaves, and settles proudly.
26-30s: the barber whisks the cape off with a matador snap, sweeps once with a broom, and looks at the waiting bench. The man lowers his newspaper, looks down at his own fern with mild concern, and the fern's fronds droop shyly. The barber, deadpan: "Next." Hold on the fern.
Audio: cape shake, two chair-pump clunks, mist puffs, comb strokes through leaves, then the scissors settling into a light waltz rhythm that becomes the score; the dog-shake rustle; broom strokes; total silence before "Next."; one page-turn after it.
Lens 40-50mm, level, unhurried; sage single-hue world with the terracotta pot and floor as the only warm accent; the joke is never acknowledged by anyone.

Art direction (Pika R3): colors soft but vibrant, true to subject, cream as the neutral instead of pure white; lighting one enormous soft source, shadows filled so every texture reads; grade natural and restrained, low-to-moderate contrast, open detailed shadows, subtle fine 35mm film grain, faint halation on highlights only, matte print-like finish, no color cast, no teal-and-orange, no HDR, no crushed blacks, no neon saturation. Every impossible event is physically real and practical-looking — NO glow, NO sparkles, NO particle magic, NO fantasy effects. All faces deadpan, composed, never mugging or winking at the joke. ABSOLUTELY NO on-screen text, no titles, no captions, no logos, no typography anywhere.
