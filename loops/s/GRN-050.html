A 30-second "lost film" written in lower case: four fixed characters (red musketeer, blue musketeer, the lady, the gardener), nine timed cuts that are almost all static, a duel that ends with both rapiers snapping, and a gardener in the background who keeps trimming, "never looking up", and still gets the girl. The style line asks for 35mm Technicolor dye-transfer, 2.35:1 anamorphic, saturated scarlet, royal blue and emerald, soft highlight bloom, visible fine grain and a diffusion filter on the lady's close-ups; the audio is a period score that cuts to silence at 21 s.

Prompt

1962 french swashbuckler costume film, a sunny afternoon in a loire valley
castle courtyard, sequence of cuts.

characters, fixed for the whole film:
the red musketeer: around thirty, shoulder-length blond hair, thin mustache,
scarlet musketeer tabard and cape, wide hat with a white plume.
the blue musketeer: around thirty-five, black beard, dark hair, royal blue
musketeer tabard and cape, wide hat with a black plume.
the lady: around twenty-five, red hair in a high updo, voluminous emerald green
gown, holding a lace fan.
the gardener: around forty, plain linen shirt, leather apron, straw hat, garden
shears, calm face.

0-4s: wide static shot of a castle courtyard with a stone fountain, the lady
watches from a stone balcony above. the two musketeers face each other and
sweep their hats in deep theatrical bows. in the background the gardener
quietly trims a round topiary bush with shears.

4-8s: cut to medium side shot, static, both draw their rapiers, the blue
musketeer says: "en garde !" they begin to fence with elegant classical
footwork, blades ringing.

8-13s: cut to wide side tracking shot following the duel across the courtyard
in one long take, lunges, parries and ripostes, the red musketeer leaps onto
the edge of the fountain and fights from there, the blue musketeer circles
below. the gardener keeps trimming in the background, never looking up.

13-16s: cut to close-up of the lady on the balcony, static, soft diffused
light, she fans herself nervously, her eyes following the blades.

16-20s: cut to medium two-shot, static, the blades lock at the hilts, the two
musketeers press face to face, mustache to beard, glaring at each other.

20-22s: cut to the same two-shot, static, they spring apart and strike at the
same instant, both rapiers snap in half with a bright metallic ping, the two
blade halves spin away and clatter on the stones. the music stops.

22-25s: cut to medium two-shot, static, both musketeers stare at the broken
hilts in their hands, then slowly look up toward the balcony with hope.

25-27s: cut to close-up of the lady, static, she sighs, snaps her fan shut and
turns away from the balcony.

27-30s: cut to wide static shot of the courtyard, the lady walks out of the
castle door, passes between the two musketeers without a glance, takes the
gardener's arm, and strolls away with him through a garden archway. the two
musketeers stand still holding their broken swords.

audio: 1960s french costume film score: lively harpsichord and baroque
strings driving the duel, rising with each exchange, cut to total silence at
21s, then a single light playful flute melody from 25s to the end. foley:
ringing steel blades, boots scraping on cobblestones, a bright snap of
breaking steel, blade pieces clattering, fountain water, birds, the snip of
garden shears. dialogue only: "en garde !"

style: shot on 35mm technicolor dye-transfer film, 2.35:1 anamorphic
widescreen, rich saturated scarlet, royal blue and emerald, warm skin tones,
soft highlight bloom, visible fine grain, light diffusion filter on the lady's
close-ups, bright sunny studio-style lighting, classical long-take fencing
choreography.

constraints: real-time speed, no slow motion, no blood, no injuries, no
stabbing, nobody gets hit, no readable text, no logos, keep the red and blue
musketeers clearly different in every shot, never swap their costumes or
hats, preserve all faces and wardrobe across cuts, the gardener is visible in
the first shot, no morphing hands, no morphing swords.
