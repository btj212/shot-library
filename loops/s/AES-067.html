Prompt

Generate a 30-second, 16:9 hand-painted gouache animated short: a man climbs a hill at golden hour and reaches a single grave at the summit.

ART STYLE — hold this exactly in every frame, it is the contract: hand-painted gouache landscape painting. Thick opaque pigment, every brush mark visible and left unblended. Directional flat-brush and palette-knife strokes that follow the slope of the terrain. Saturated emerald and spring-green hillside against cool slate-blue and lavender haze; white flecked wildflowers scattered through the grass; deep forest-green shadow masses. Strong atmospheric perspective — distant cliff walls flatten into pale blue mist. NO outlines and NO line work at all: form comes entirely from brush mark and value. Matte pigment throughout, no gloss, no gradient blending, no airbrush, no digital smoothing, no photorealism, no text or watermarks. The figure stays small and simplified, a painted silhouette rather than a rendered character.

ANIMATION STYLE — this is equally binding: hand-painted frame-by-frame traditional animation running at 12 frames per second, on twos. Every frame is a separately painted gouache painting, so the surface never sits still — the whole image BOILS. Every 2 to 4 frames the brush texture, edge placement and pigment grain visibly shift and re-form, because a human repainted them. Outlines of hills, clouds and the figure wobble and breathe frame to frame. Colours flicker very slightly between adjacent frames as pigment density varies. This boil must be constant and present in every single shot, including the still ones. Do NOT smooth, interpolate, stabilise or motion-blur between frames — the judder and the boil ARE the style. No 24fps smoothness, no CG tweening.

CHARACTER: Image 1 is the world and the style authority. The climber is a lone man in a dark coat, small in frame, painted as a simplified silhouette with no facial detail except in the two close-ups described below.

Shot 1 [0-4s]: THE START. Wide. The man begins climbing the steep green slope, tiny against the vast misty valley and pale cliff walls behind him. Late golden light rakes across the grass.
Shot 2 [4-7s]: B-ROLL — GRASS. Extreme close-up. Wind ruffles through the grass blades and white wildflowers, stems bending and springing back, painted marks reshaping with every frame.
Shot 3 [7-10s]: THE CLIMB. Medium-wide. He works his way up the slope, leaning into the gradient, one hand steadying against the ground. The valley falls away behind him.
Shot 4 [10-12s]: B-ROLL — BIRDS. Medium shot of the pale sky. A loose scatter of birds crosses the frame, painted as quick dark strokes, wheeling once.
Shot 5 [12-15s]: B-ROLL — BREATH. Extreme close-up on the climber's mouth and jaw. He is breathing hard and open-mouthed, warm breath faintly visible in the cooling air, a strand of hair stuck to damp skin.
Shot 6 [15-18s]: THE LAST PUSH. He crests the final ridge, pushing off his own knee with one hand, head down, then slowly straightens up.
Shot 7 [18-22s]: THE REVEAL. Reverse angle. At the summit stands a single weathered stone grave marker, alone on the grass — and behind it the sun is setting low, the whole sky glowing in soft amber, apricot and rose, warm light haloing the edges of the stone. He stands motionless.
Shot 8 [22-26s]: THE SIT. Wide shot. He holds still for a beat, just taking it in, the wind moving the grass around his legs. Then he walks slowly forward and sits down on the ground in front of the grave marker, facing it. The sky glows on, gold fading gently into rose.
Shot 9 [26-30s]: THE GREETING. Close-up on his face in three-quarter profile, the only time we see him clearly — weathered, tired, eyes glistening, the warm evening light soft on his cheek. He looks at the stone, and a small, tender smile finds him. Quietly, warmly, his voice low and catching slightly, he says: "Hey, it's been a while." He breathes out and keeps his eyes on the stone. Hold on his face to the final frame.

CAMERA: patient and mostly locked, letting the painted frames do the work — a slow drift with him on the climbing shots, static for the three b-roll inserts, a steady hold on the wide of him sitting, and a slow, gentle push-in on his face for the final line that settles and holds. No handheld shake, no fast moves.

AUDIO: no music until the reveal at 18s, then a single sustained cello line that swells gently, softens to almost nothing under his words, and resolves on the final frame. Wind moving through grass, boots on soil, laboured breathing in the breath close-up, distant birdcalls, and the deep quiet of high open ground. The only dialogue in the entire video is his single quiet line at the end, in his own voice, low, gentle and sentimental: "Hey, it's been a while."
