Prompt

Create a 30-second ultra-realistic cinematic comedy set inside a 1997 Hollywood science-fiction film studio. The film crew is auditioning original alien creatures for an upcoming blockbuster. Make it feel like authentic archival behind-the-scenes footage from a real 1990s production, with practical creature suits, analog film grain, warm tungsten lighting, vintage cinema cameras, cables across the floor, and crew members wearing period-appropriate clothes.

IMPORTANT EDITING DIRECTION: This is a fast-paced sequence of distinct audition segments. Use clear hard cuts, brief reaction shots, changes in camera distance, and different camera positions for every candidate. Each audition must feel like a separate take recorded by a documentary crew. Do not use the same camera angle, framing, entrance, or transition repeatedly. Maintain visual continuity within the studio while varying the composition and action.

SHOT 1 — ESTABLISHING SHOT (0–3 seconds):
Wide documentary shot of a busy 1997 Hollywood soundstage. A director sits in a canvas-backed chair beside a vintage film camera. An assistant checks a clipboard and calls, “Next candidate!” Crew members turn toward the audition area.

HARD CUT.

SHOT 2 — CANDIDATE ONE (3–8 seconds):
Medium-wide frontal shot of a short, fluffy purple alien standing under bright audition lights. It attempts a terrifying monster roar, but the sound comes out like a tiny squeak. Cut immediately to a close-up of the director slowly lowering his clipboard in disappointment. The alien gives an embarrassed little wave.

HARD CUT TO A NEW CAMERA POSITION.

SHOT 3 — CANDIDATE TWO (8–13 seconds):
Low-angle side shot of a slender silver alien entering from behind a rolling studio flat. It attempts to demonstrate its supernatural powers and unexpectedly floats upside down, its legs gently kicking in the air. Cut to a crew member looking upward, then to the director checking his watch. The moment should feel spontaneous and physically believable.

HARD CUT.

SHOT 4 — CANDIDATE THREE (13–18 seconds):
Over-the-shoulder shot from behind the director, revealing a tiny insect-like alien standing in the center of the enormous soundstage. The creature performs an intense, intimidating pose. The director leans forward, intrigued. Suddenly, the alien releases a cloud of harmless glittering dust. Cut to the crew shielding their faces and the director brushing glitter off his jacket.

HARD CUT TO A WIDER FRAME.

SHOT 5 — FINAL CANDIDATE (18–25 seconds):
A static wide shot faces a massive studio entrance. The doors slowly swing open, revealing an enormous, frightening original alien creature with textured charcoal skin, elongated limbs, and intricate biological details. It ducks beneath the doorway and takes two heavy steps onto the stage. Cut to a close-up of the director's astonished face, then a low-angle shot emphasizing the creature's immense size. The crew stands completely silent.

HARD CUT TO A TIGHTER SHOT.

SHOT 6 — COMEDIC PAYOFF (25–30 seconds):
Close-up of the enormous alien preparing to unleash a terrifying roar. Its jaws open wide, but it produces an absurdly tiny squeaky sneeze. Cut to loose audition papers flying across the room, followed by the director calmly turning toward his assistant and saying, “That's our star.” End on a brief wide shot of the bewildered crew and the giant alien standing proudly.

CINEMATOGRAPHY:
Authentic 1990s 35mm film appearance, restrained handheld movement, natural motion blur, realistic focus changes, slightly imperfect framing, warm practical lights, subtle film grain, and believable shadows. Mix wide establishing shots, medium audition shots, low angles, over-the-shoulder shots, and close-up reaction shots.

SOUND:
Natural studio ambience, footsteps, camera mechanisms, quiet crew chatter, creature vocalizations, and precisely timed dialogue. Preserve short pauses between jokes. Dialogue must come from the correct characters and remain synchronized.

CONTINUITY:
The same director, assistant, crew, studio architecture, and lighting setup remain consistent throughout. Each alien has a distinct original design and behavior. Every hard cut must introduce a new composition or meaningful reaction. Keep all creatures photorealistic, with convincing physical weight and movement.

AVOID:
Cartoon aesthetics, glossy CGI, repeated framing, repetitive entrances, long uninterrupted takes, random scene changes, inconsistent characters, subtitles, watermarks, logos, and modern production equipment.
